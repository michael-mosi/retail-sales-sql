# Business Insights | Duka Smart Electronics

## 1. Accessories make a small share of sales but a large share of profit (Q16, Q33, Q42)

**Finding.** Mobile Phones (28.7% of revenue) and Computing (27.3%) bring in the most money, but
their margins are thin: 10.5% and 10.6%. Electronics earns 9.9% and Home Appliances 11.4%.
**Accessories are only 4.5% of revenue (KSh 1.08M), yet they earn a 42.4% margin and 15.8% of all
profit**. That is KSh 456,746, about the same as Home Appliances (KSh 458,034) on roughly a quarter
of the sales. Mobile Phones is the most profitable category overall at KSh 728,761 (Q46). At product
level the big-ticket items are thinnest of all: Hotpoint Refrigerator 4.0%, Samsung 55in TV 4.6%,
iPhone 13 6.2%. Chargers and earbuds earn about 50%.

**Why it matters.** Each shilling of accessory sales makes about four times the profit of a shilling
of phone or laptop sales. Phones and laptops bring customers in; accessories are where the profit is.

**Recommendation.** Offer an accessory bundle with every phone, laptop and TV sale: a charger,
earbuds or a mouse and keyboard. Make it the default at checkout and track the share of phone and
laptop orders that include an accessory. If attach rates rise by even a few points, profit grows
without any extra customers.

## 2. Heavy discounts are turning sales into losses (Q33, business rules)

**Finding.** Margin falls steadily as the discount rises: 17.5% at no discount, 13.4% at 5%, 6.7% at
10%, 2.5% at 15%, then **−2.2% at 20% and −12.0% at 25%**. **62 completed orders were sold below
cost, losing KSh 141,979 in total.** The worst cases are big-ticket items with already thin
margins: Hotpoint Refrigerator at 20 to 25% off, iPhone 13 at 15 to 20% off, and Lenovo IdeaPad at 20%
off. Altogether, discounts cost KSh 1.52M in revenue on completed orders.

**Why it matters.** Any discount larger than the product's margin loses money on every unit. On a
fridge with a 9% gross margin, a 15% discount means losing money on the sale.

**Recommendation.** Cap discounts per product at its margin: no more than 5% on refrigerators, TVs
and iPhones, and up to 20% on accessories. Require a manager's approval above the cap. Re-run Q33
monthly to check that the margin on discounted orders stays positive.

## 3. Revenue depends on a small group of customers (Q45, Q24, Q46)

**Finding.** The **High Value** band is the top 20% of buyers, each having spent **at least
KSh 318,011**. That is **27 customers bringing in 62.5% of all revenue** (KSh 15.1M). The Low Value
band, spending under KSh 71,197, has 53 customers and brings in just 6.3%. The top five customers are
Brian Chebet (KSh 848,119), Peter Ndungu (KSh 790,230), Lucy Njoroge (KSh 767,881), Nancy Korir
(KSh 702,546) and Purity Ochieng (KSh 698,922). **All five are tagged "Retail".**

**Why it matters.** Losing a handful of these 27 customers would hurt more than losing all 53 Low
Value ones.

**Recommendation.** Give every High Value customer a named contact, early access to new stock and a
loyalty reward. Report the revenue from this group to management every month as a key risk measure.

**Why these thresholds.** Spending is heavily skewed, from KSh 1,899 to KSh 848,119, so round-number
cut-offs picked in advance would be arbitrary. Percentiles of buying customers (80th and 40th) fit
the data and follow the 80/20 pattern. The 18 customers with no completed purchase are reported
separately so they don't pull the cut-offs down.

## 4. The "VIP" label does not match actual value (Q27, Q28, Q45)

**Finding.** VIP customers do spend more on average: KSh 253,252 per buying VIP, against KSh 197,959
for Corporate and KSh 163,575 for Retail. But **only 5 of the 27 High Value customers are VIPs, while
15 are Retail**, and 9 of the top 10 spenders are Retail (Q24). Four VIPs sit in the Low Value band.
VIPs order Home Appliances most often: 35 of their 145 orders (Q28).

**Why it matters.** VIP perks are going partly to low spenders, while the shop's best customers get
no recognition and could easily move to a competitor.

**Recommendation.** Assign the VIP tier from actual spending (the Q45 bands), reviewed every quarter,
instead of a label given at registration. Use Home Appliances, the category VIPs order most, as the
focus of VIP promotions.

## 5. Sales are growing fast, with a December peak and a January slump (Q31, Q38, Q40, Q46)

**Finding.** Revenue for January to September 2026 was **KSh 12.83M, up 86% on KSh 6.89M** for the same
months of 2025. Orders rose to 419 in the first nine months of 2026, against 381 for the whole of
2025. The three best months were **August 2026 (KSh 2.24M), September 2026 (KSh 2.09M) and June 2026
(KSh 2.04M)**. December 2025 (KSh 2.03M) was a close fourth. The **lowest-revenue month was
January 2026 at KSh 322,433**, a fall of about 84% from December, followed by a recovery that
reached KSh 1.65M by April.

**Why it matters.** The business is growing and needs stock and cash ready for it. The swing from
December to January is predictable, so it can be planned for.

**Recommendation.** Order stock for December 6 to 8 weeks ahead. Plan a January campaign, such as a
back-to-school offer on laptops and accessories or M-Pesa instalment payments, to soften the slump.
Use the `monthly_sales_summary` view (Q43) as the monthly management report.
